# Pricing method

## Expected claims cost

One row is treated as a policy on an equal-exposure basis. The recorded loss is an aggregate policy amount; individual-claim severity data are unavailable. Frequency models use claim counts. Severity calculations respect the number of claims represented by each policy row.

The candidates include Poisson frequency models, Gamma severity models, spline terms, boosting, hurdle models and a Tweedie alternative. A constant benchmark remains part of the comparison. Large losses remain in evaluation. Body-tail challengers cap only the fitting component and restore a pooled excess estimate.

Model structure and calibration are compared within two repeats of five grouped development folds. Identical risk profiles stay together. Calibration uses an inner sample. The simplicity rule uses a repeated-fold one-standard-error heuristic; overlapping folds do not supply an independent confidence interval.

## Monetary allowance and quote

The quote adds expected claims cost, fixed expense and a monetary allowance, then allows for variable expenses and tax. Flat and tiered allowances are compared with continuous transitions around tier boundaries. The transition regions are not constant. A fixed amount also has a larger relative effect on cheaper risks, so it is not automatically fairer than a percentage.

The allowance is a pricing choice. It is not a fitted regulatory capital charge. Tail simulations and common-event stresses examine sensitivity; they cannot estimate unknown catastrophes from this dataset.

## Commercial decisions

Competitor price levels, risk tilts, elasticity and selection are assumptions. The scenario grid checks volume and expected contribution jointly. If no action meets both constraints, the output reports infeasibility. It does not choose an action merely because it attracts more policies.

## Before operational use

Confirm exposure and loss valuation, establish coverage and currency, obtain actual expenses and conversion evidence, and validate on independent data. Review rating factors, customer outcomes and local requirements in the intended jurisdiction. The existing study offers review questions and diagnostics, not a regulatory approval.
